# Mansion battle lights and a two-view cutscene (#212) — 2026-10-04

Issue #212 (v0.8.15, RTX 4060, D3D12, DLSS Balanced, AO on) reports two things that flicker only with TAA or DLSS: the ground in a battle at Old Sorceress' Mansion, and block shadows on Jansen in a cutscene.

## Battle: unmapped per-light floor passes

The player's log has two runtime suspects when the Courtyard (`ge7_0_scrw`) battle starts: VS `9bdef27080ca3ab4` with PS `d122f0139a58bdac`, and VS `e810cfacc107fd3c` with PS `c44ebbbc0207b5a9`. Both are `after_jittered_geometry` over the slot-7 floor draw `4053f2a21dbb92dd` with the same world and camera, no depth write and a GEQUAL depth test. A local battle entered from the Entrance Hall logged a third pair, e810 with PS `a800980dfc9e4efe`, which lights the floor around an enemy.

These are UE3 per-light passes. c7–c10 reach only `oPos` and the `o4` copy (9bde is e810 with an extra fetch94 scalar). Each PS samples tex0, the light attenuation, at `o4.xy/w` through `XeConst(0)` and multiplies it into the light, like the f6131 `2078`/`4013` floor light. `triage_suspect.py` holds them as screen samplers, and e810 stays out of the VS-wide map because of its other partners. Unjittered over the jittered floor depth, the GEQUAL test dropped light per pixel every frame.

The three exact pairs go into a new `ScreenLightPairs` table (slot 7, ordinary anchor and motion policy). The tex0 lookup moves with the raster jitter, so no PS constant changes. `triage_suspect.py` reads the table as mapped.

## Cutscene: a second full scene view with its own camera

Searching the event subtitles for the line in the screenshot finds `rt_084.sub`: RT_084B "Tolten Becomes 45th King" on disc 2 (Scenario Jump Page007, row 10 from the debug map). It reproduced locally on a build that already had #183's fix.

An F1 capture of Jansen's close-up shows the frame drawing the scene twice with two cameras: draws 270–1140 with the first camera, then draws 1217–3170 with Jansen's. `SceneObservation` anchors the first camera. The second view's depth writers anchor themselves and jitter, but its shadow volumes (97f0), projections (99c2) and lights (c84c), which never write depth, compared against the first camera: about 800 `CameraMismatch` draws, the #183 failure through a different door. Both views use the jitter viewport, so #183's viewport check does not apply.

`SceneObservation` now remembers each camera that anchored a jittered draw in the frame (up to four) and each full-extent depth resolve of their allocations. A draw without its own anchor uses the observed camera its VP matches; a shadow projection may sample the depth resolve of its own view. The first camera stays the frame anchor, and two views still reject the SR composition as before.
